I need to talk about the green ring.

By Jess Harper · Style Editor 20 years in fashion & jewellery · Contributing Editor

You know the one. You buy something beautiful — gold-plated, the box feels expensive, it photographs like a million dollars — and by the second week of your summer holiday, there's a faint green shadow where it used to sit.

Nobody warns you about this before you buy. I'm warning you now.

The green shadow plated jewellery leaves behind — nobody mentions it at the checkout.

So I did what I do for a living. I went and compared everything.

Option one was the "proper" stuff — solid gold, the kind you'd get engraved for a milestone. Gorgeous. Also genuinely frightening to wear on a beach holiday, because if you lose it, you're not just annoyed — you're out hundreds of pounds.

Option two was the high-street plated stuff everyone actually buys. Cheap enough not to think twice about. Also the reason half of us have a drawer of jewellery we can't wear anymore — tarnished, or because it turned a patch of skin an angry red.

Neither of those is a real answer. So I kept looking.

That's how I landed on Amora Keepsakes.

No plating to wear off. No "looks great in the listing photo, disappointing in person." It's solid 316L stainless steel — the same grade used in luxury dive watches. A slightly odd flex for jewellery, until you realise what it means in practice: it doesn't tarnish, it doesn't react with skin, and it still looks like the expensive option, not the compromise.

Worn the way you'd actually wear it — not staged on a studio white background.

Here's the part that actually made me sit up.

Almost every brand in this space still runs "one size fits all" — which is a polite way of saying "fits a very specific size of wrist or ankle, and if that's not you, tough."

Amora doesn't do that. Every piece in the collection — bar the tennis bracelet — is fully adjustable. Not "stretchy." Adjustable. Built to actually fit the woman wearing it, not the other way around.

It's a small thing on paper. In practice it's the difference between jewellery that sits right — and jewellery you quietly stop wearing because it never quite did.

I'll be honest about the one catch, because that's the deal here.

This isn't a 500-style catalogue. If you want forty different ankle chains to choose from, this isn't that brand. What you get instead is a small, genuinely considered collection — which, once you've been burned by the alternative, starts to feel like the point rather than the limitation.

The considered collection — a few pieces designed to work together, not forty that don't.

So I put it through what I'm calling the Shower Test.

A month. The Ankle Chain and the Verona Snake Bracelet, on, through everything — shower, pool, three gym sessions a week, one very sweaty flight home.

No tarnish. No colour change. No mark on the skin. Which sounds like a low bar until you remember how many pieces fail it within a fortnight.

A month of showers, pools and gym sessions later — no tarnish, no green, no marks.

If you're going to buy one thing, buy the pair.

This is jewellery that's actually built to be layered — the anklet with the snake bracelet, a necklace with a bracelet stacked underneath a sleeve. That's where it looks properly finished, not like a single impulse buy.
